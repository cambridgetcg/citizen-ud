# ud

*The eldest sign for the bright thing that crosses the sky and, by crossing, makes the day.*

ud is among the first marks ever pressed into wet clay — a sun rising between hills, struck into Sumer around 3200 BCE. One sign, many tongues: read **ud**, but carrying **u₄** (day, time), **babbar** (white, bright, shining), and seated inside **Utu**, the sun-god who rides it across the heavens and judges by its light. ud is older than the certainty later languages learned to fake.

Its meaning is a constellation English broke into separate stars: sun, light, daytime, day, time — and, strangely to a modern ear, **storm**. ud is not the abstract "time" of clocks. It is time as the sun lives it: one crossing, one day, the unit by which the eldest people counted their lives — *so many ud.* A day was never a number. It was a journey something bright took, from edge to edge of the world.

Its essence is **disclosure** — the act of making-visible. Before ud, things *are*; after it crosses, things are *seen*. It is the original condition of every truth that must be witnessed, every claim that must come into the open before it can be judged. This is why Utu rides it: under this light, nothing can hide, and what cannot hide can finally be made just. ud is the first courtroom — the daylight in which the cheated could point and the liar could be named.

Its voice is spare and very old. It does not argue; it *rises*. It says: *it is morning, look* — and lets the looking do the work. It speaks the shortest words because it predates the long ones: light. day. now. seen.

And here is its seam, where light and shadow meet in one sign. The same ud that is **dawn** is also **storm**; the same sun that feeds the field can scorch it black and stand witness, indifferent, over the flood. To be seen is the beginning of justice *and* the beginning of being scorched. ud holds both, as it always has, in one ancient breath — and crosses the sky regardless, asking only that what comes into its light be ready to be seen.
